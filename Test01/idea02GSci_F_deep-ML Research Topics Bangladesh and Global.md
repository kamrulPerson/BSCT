# **Advancing Resource-Constrained Computer Vision: High-Impact Research Trajectories in Multi-Object Tracking, Edge AI, and Adversarial Robustness**

The deployment of robust Computer Vision (CV) and Machine Learning (ML) models in the Global South presents unique algorithmic and computational challenges. Traffic ecosystems in metropolises such as Dhaka are characterized by extreme heterogeneity, a lack of lane discipline, dense congestion, and the presence of non-standard vehicles like battery-operated auto-rickshaws and hand carts1. Concurrently, local researchers and edge-deployment systems often operate under heavily constrained computational budgets, typically relying on accessible hardware such as the Nvidia T4 GPU, which provides 16GB of VRAM and Turing-architecture Tensor Cores optimized for FP16 mixed-precision operations. Conducting top-tier, Q1-publishable research under these constraints requires pivoting away from brute-force scaling—such as massive transformer architectures or foundational vision-language models—and instead prioritizing "cheap novelty." This paradigm emphasizes innovations in algorithmic efficiency, mathematical reformulations of loss functions, cross-modal signal processing, and targeted applications relevant to unstructured environments.  
A systematic synthesis of the 2024–2026 literature reveals high-potential intersections across Tiny Machine Learning (TinyML), Evidential Deep Learning (EDL), Multi-Object Tracking (MOT), and Adversarial Security. The following comprehensive analysis distills these domains to formulate three highly novel, computationally lightweight, and mathematically rigorous research frameworks tailored for an undergraduate research team operating on a 4-to-6-month timeline.

## **Synthesis of the 2024–2026 Literature and Thematic Intersections**

### **The Evolution of Multi-Object Tracking on the Edge**

Tracking-by-detection remains the dominant paradigm for real-time MOT. Architectures like ByteTrack have optimized the association phase by utilizing both high-confidence and low-confidence detection boxes to recover heavily occluded objects, achieving real-time performance (up to 30+ frames per second) on T4-class hardware3. However, ByteTrack and its derivatives, including DeepSORT and BoT-SORT, rely almost exclusively on deterministic bounding box outputs and spatial Intersection over Union (IoU) metrics5. In highly unstructured and chaotic traffic, deterministic trackers suffer from severe identity fragmentation, quantified as Identity Switches (IDSW). This occurs because standard convolutional detectors cannot mathematically quantify their own spatial uncertainty when bounding boxes overlap in dense crowds or traffic jams.  
Recent advancements have attempted to resolve these tracking bottlenecks via TinyML modular networks. For instance, the Single-Prediction Network (SPENT) and modular association networks (SANT/MANTa) replace the traditional heuristic Kalman Filter and the Hungarian matching algorithm with task-specific Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) containing fewer than 50,000 trainable parameters7. These miniature networks operate seamlessly within real-time automotive constraints while reducing prediction Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) by over 50% compared to linear Kalman dynamics7. While these ML-based prediction modules improve trajectory estimation, they still do not inherently quantify the epistemic uncertainty of the visual detector itself.

### **The Emergence of Evidential Deep Learning (EDL)**

Traditional methods for uncertainty quantification in computer vision, such as Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs) or Monte Carlo (MC) Dropout, require multiple forward passes through the network. This drastically increases inference latency, rendering them unviable for real-time edge tracking9. Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) bypasses this computational bottleneck by quantifying uncertainty in a single forward pass9. Grounded in Dempster-Shafer Theory of Evidence (DST) and Subjective Logic (SL), EDL models the output of a neural network not as a definitive point-estimate probability, but as the concentration parameters ($\alpha$) of a Dirichlet distribution11.  
The Dirichlet probability density function is mathematically defined as:

\$\$Dir(p\\vert{}\\alpha) \= \\frac{\\Gamma(\\sum\_{i=1}^K \\alpha\_i)}{\\prod\_{i=1}^K \\Gamma(\\alpha\_i)} \\prod\_{i=1}^K p\_i^{\\alpha\_i \- 1}\$\$  
Where the evidence ${e}_{i}\geq 0$ generated by the network relates directly to the Dirichlet parameter via the equation ${\alpha }_{i}={e}_{i}+1$. The epistemic uncertainty—which represents the model's lack of knowledge due to out-of-distribution data or severe occlusion—is directly quantifiable as $u=\frac{K}{\sum\limits_{}^{}{\alpha }_{i}}$11. Applying this theoretical framework to bounding box regression and classification provides a mathematically elegant, computationally inexpensive method to quantify spatial uncertainty in chaotic traffic streams.

### **Adversarial Robustness and Compressed-Domain Security**

As MOT systems are increasingly deployed in security and autonomous driving, their vulnerability to adversarial attacks has become a critical area of research. Adversarial attacks against MOT systems have evolved significantly from digital pixel perturbations to deployable physical patches. Frameworks such as PapMOT have recently demonstrated that physical adversarial patches can successfully induce Identity Transfer (ID-Transfer) attacks, explicitly causing the tracker to swap the identities of moving objects or drop them entirely15. The ADVTRAJ attack further exploits trajectory optimization to confuse the Kalman filter's association matrix in physical space16.  
Defending against these sophisticated physical attacks using standard RGB-based adversarial training is computationally prohibitive for edge devices. Consequently, researchers have begun exploring compressed-domain video analysis. By leveraging H.264/AVC or HEVC bitstreams, systems can extract pre-computed Motion Vectors (MVs) directly from the video macroblocks without fully decoding the video into the RGB pixel space17. The MVTrack architecture has demonstrated that these motion vectors can achieve ultrafast, appearance-free tracking17. Because MVs represent raw physical kinematic data generated by the camera's hardware encoder, they are inherently immune to RGB-focused adversarial patches, presenting a highly novel, zero-GPU-overhead mechanism for cross-modal adversarial defense.

### **Privacy-Preserving Edge Deployments via Network Pruning**

The deployment of surveillance and tracking systems in smart cities raises significant privacy concerns. Transmitting raw video feeds to centralized cloud servers exposes Personally Identifiable Information (PII), such as faces and license plates, violating privacy frameworks21. Processing data entirely on the edge mitigates this risk but is hindered by the memory and compute limits of devices like the Nvidia Jetson or constrained T4 instances. Recent literature emphasizes Multi-Stage Pruning (MSP) frameworks, such as MSP-Edge, which combine unstructured sparsification with deployment-oriented neuron reconstruction to compress models like YOLOv8n by up to 58.2% in weights and 61.7% in FLOPs, without sacrificing dense-crowd detection accuracy23. This enables sophisticated tracking pipelines to operate strictly on the edge, ensuring data minimization and privacy preservation by design.

## **Research Framework 1: Evidential Uncertainty in Global South Traffic**

### **Title**

**Evidential-ByteTrack: Dirichlet-Based Uncertainty Quantification for Multi-Object Tracking in Unstructured Global South Traffic**

### **Foundational Concept and Real-World Application**

Consider a highly congested intersection in Dhaka where a battery-operated auto-rickshaw is partially occluded by a larger local bus. A standard tracking pipeline (e.g., YOLOv11 coupled with ByteTrack) will output a fluctuating, low-confidence bounding box for the partially hidden rickshaw. The rigid Hungarian matching algorithm, relying on Intersection over Union (IoU), will likely drop the track and assign a completely new Identity (causing an IDSW) once the rickshaw reappears from behind the bus. Under the proposed Evidential-ByteTrack framework, the detector does not just output a bounding box; it outputs a Dirichlet distribution estimating high *epistemic uncertainty* for that specific spatial region. The tracking algorithm reads this uncertainty metric and temporarily relaxes its spatial IoU matching constraints. It holds the trajectory state in memory, recognizing that the model is mathematically uncertain, and seamlessly preserves the vehicle's identity upon its reappearance.

### **Targeted Problem and Primary Beneficiaries**

Unstructured traffic, particularly in the Global South, violates the strict linear motion assumptions of standard Kalman filters. Heavy, unpredictable occlusions cause severe identity fragmentation, rendering automated traffic monitoring—such as congestion footprinting, violation detection, and vehicle counting—highly inaccurate1. The primary beneficiaries of this research include municipal traffic authorities in developing nations, urban planners aiming to quantify the impact of non-standard vehicles, and the broader autonomous driving research community focusing on robust perception in non-lane-based environments.

### **Literature Gap and Core Novelty**

Current state-of-the-art trackers attempt to manage occlusion by integrating heavy appearance features (Re-ID models) or utilizing dual-threshold low-confidence box association, as seen in ByteTrack3. However, Re-ID models fail catastrophically on uniform vehicles (e.g., identical yellow CNG auto-rickshaws), and ByteTrack lacks a mathematically grounded metric for spatial uncertainty. While Evidential Deep Learning has been successfully applied to image classification, medical imaging, and out-of-distribution detection25, its integration into the fundamental cost matrix of real-time MOT for spatial trajectory association remains largely unexplored.  
The core novelty—and the source of "cheap compute" innovation—is the introduction of "Evidential-IoU" (E-IoU). By appending a highly lightweight evidential head to a YOLOv9-tiny or YOLOv11n architecture, the model learns to predict Dirichlet parameters in a *single forward pass*. This entirely avoids the massive computational overhead associated with Deep Ensembles or MC Dropout. The innovation lies in modifying the tracking assignment cost matrix to utilize epistemic uncertainty dynamically, achieving state-of-the-art identity preservation without requiring any additional GPU FLOPs during the inference phase.

### **Execution Strategy and Mathematical Implementation**

The execution of this framework requires precise modifications to both the detection head and the tracking association logic, perfectly suited for a team with strong Python and PyTorch capabilities.

> 1. **Evidential Detector Modification:** The detection head of a lightweight YOLO model is modified at the source code level. Instead of the traditional Softmax or Sigmoid activation layers, a Softplus activation is applied to the final output tensors to ensure the generation of non-negative evidence values ${e}_{i}$.  
> 2. **Loss Formulation:** The model must be trained using the Mean Squared Error Evidential Loss (${L}_{MSE-EDL}$) combined with a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalty. This penalty is crucial as it shrinks evidence for out-of-distribution or heavily occluded objects, forcing the model to express uncertainty10. The implementation follows the mathematical structure: \$\$ L\_{EDL} \= \\sum\_{j=1}^K (y\_j \- \\frac{\\alpha\_j}{S})^2 \+ \\frac{\\alpha\_j(S \- \\alpha\_j)}{S^2(S+1)} \+ \\lambda\_{KL} KL\[Dir(p\\vert{}\\tilde{\\alpha}) \\vert{} \\vert{} Dir(p\\vert{}1)\] \$\$  
> 3. **Cost Matrix Integration:** The predicted epistemic uncertainty $u$ is extracted and utilized to dynamically scale the Kalman Filter's measurement noise covariance matrix $R$. Furthermore, the standard IoU cost matrix in the Hungarian algorithm is replaced with an uncertainty-aware variant: $Cost(i,j)=(1-IoU(i,j))\times \exp (-\beta \cdot {u}_{i})$. This formula heavily penalizes confident mismatches but allows for flexible spatial matching when the network is highly uncertain about a bounding box's exact boundaries.  
> 4. **Hardware Constraints:** All training and inference modifications must be engineered to run natively on a single T4 GPU. This requires strict adherence to FP16 Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP) training in PyTorch to maximize the 16GB VRAM utility.

### **Performance Estimates and Risk Mitigation**

It is projected that integrating Evidential-IoU will reduce IDSW by 15-20% in dense, highly occluded datasets like DriveIndia. This reduction directly correlates to boosting the IDF1 score by an estimated 3-5 absolute percentage points compared to the baseline ByteTrack architecture. The computational overhead is estimated to be $<2$ ms per frame on a T4 GPU, safely maintaining real-time throughput ($>30$ FPS).  
A primary risk involves training instability; EDL loss functions can suffer from early-stage gradient explosion due to the KL divergence term. This is mitigated by implementing a delayed annealing coefficient ${\lambda }_{t}=\min\limits_{}(1,t/10)$ to gradually introduce the KL penalty over the first 10 epochs of training10. Additionally, DriveIndia features heavily imbalanced classes (e.g., an overrepresentation of cars versus animals)28. This requires the integration of Focal Loss principles within the evidential framework to prevent majority-class dominance.

## **Research Framework 2: Cross-Modal Adversarial Defense in Traffic Surveillance**

### **Foundational Concept and Real-World Application**

Imagine a scenario where a malicious actor places a specially crafted printed adversarial patch—generated via a framework like PapMOT—on a targeted vehicle or license plate to evade a city's automated tracking system15. As the vehicle moves through the CCTV frame, the RGB-based deep learning detector is visually fooled. The tracker either drops the bounding box entirely or initiates an "ID-Transfer" attack, swapping the vehicle's identity with a nearby pedestrian, effectively rendering the targeted vehicle invisible to the security network16. However, the proposed cross-modal system detects this physical movement independently by reading the raw H.264 motion vectors directly from the video stream's compressed metadata. Recognizing a severe mathematical discrepancy between the blank RGB neural network output and the highly active motion vectors, the system flags the adversarial attack in real-time and utilizes the motion vectors to maintain the vehicle's trajectory.

### **Targeted Problem and Primary Beneficiaries**

Adversarial robustness in both 2D and 3D vision systems represents a critical, often overlooked vulnerability for autonomous driving and smart-city surveillance infrastructure35. Physical patch attacks are inexpensive to produce, easily deployed in the physical world, and devastatingly effective against standard YOLO and FairMOT architectures. The primary beneficiaries of resolving this vulnerability include national security agencies, automated tolling and traffic enforcement systems, and autonomous vehicle safety validation frameworks that require highly secure perception modules.

### **Literature Gap and Core Novelty**

Existing defenses against adversarial patches focus almost exclusively on adversarial training or input image denoising within the RGB pixel domain36. These defense mechanisms are computationally exorbitant—often requiring multiple heavy diffusion or generative passes—and generally fail to generalize to new, unseen patch designs. Utilizing the video codec's compressed domain metadata, specifically motion vectors (MVs), as an independent, physics-grounded modality to verify RGB neural network outputs represents a highly novel, underexplored gap in the MOT security literature.  
The core "cheap compute" novelty lies in leveraging tools like FFmpeg or PyAV to extract H.264/HEVC motion vectors. This avoids deep learning entirely for the verification step; it is a CPU-bound, deterministic parsing operation of video macroblocks18. The innovation is the construction of a dual-stream verification architecture where a zero-FLOP kinematic stream acts as an independent auditor for a vulnerable, high-FLOP RGB tracking stream, achieving state-of-the-art adversarial defense with negligible computational overhead.

### **Execution Strategy and Algorithmic Implementation**

This framework heavily leverages C++ and Python integration to bridge video decoding with deep learning tensors.

> 1. **Simulating the Adversarial Attack:** The team will utilize the open-source PapMOT framework to generate physical adversarial patches explicitly tailored to disrupt ByteTrack and FairMOT identity associations15. These patches will be applied digitally to frames within the Indian Driving Dataset (IDD) to simulate a physical attack.  
> 2. **Compressed Domain Parsing:** Develop a highly optimized C++ or Python wrapper to extract the motion vector field $(dx,dy)$ for every $16\times 16$ macroblock found in the Predictive (P-frames) and Bidirectional (B-frames) of the H.264 video stream18.  
> 3. **Cross-Modal Verification Filter:** For each tracked object $i$, the algorithm compares the bounding box centroid velocity calculated by the RGB Kalman filter (${v}_{RGB}$) against the mean motion vector of the macroblocks contained within that specific bounding box (${v}_{MV}$).  
> 4. **Anomaly Resolution and State Recovery:** Define a mathematical anomaly score: \$S\_a \= \\vert{}\\vert{}v\_{RGB} \- \\beta \\cdot v\_{MV}\\vert{}\\vert{}\_2\$. If ${S}_{a}>\tau$ (a calibrated scalar threshold), the system assumes an adversarial perturbation or a severe un-modeled occlusion is occurring. The tracker immediately suspends RGB bounding box updates and propagates the tracking state purely using the compressed-domain motion vectors, heavily inspired by the appearance-free MVTrack paradigm17.

### **Performance Estimates and Risk Mitigation**

Without defense, frameworks like PapMOT achieve a TASR of over 60% on robust trackers like ByteTrack15. Because the proposed cross-modal verification relies on raw codec physics, it is mathematically isolated from RGB pixel noise. Consequently, it is estimated to reduce TASR to below 15% and recover the IDF1 tracking score to within 90% of the baseline unattacked performance. This requires less than 5% of the T4 GPU's overhead, as the heavy parsing is explicitly CPU-delegated.  
A significant engineering risk is I-Frame Dependency. H.264 motion vectors are absent in Intra-coded (I) frames, leaving a temporary gap in the MV verification stream. This is mitigated by programming a brief hold-over memory state in the tracking logic that interpolates expected kinematics across I-frames. Furthermore, moving cameras (e.g., drones or dashcams) generate global motion vectors, confusing object tracking. To mitigate this, a background motion compensation filter must be implemented, which calculates and subtracts the median global motion vector from the local object vectors19.

## **Research Framework 3: Privacy-Preserving Edge MOT via Neuron Reconstruction**

### **Foundational Concept and Real-World Application**

In urban surveillance scenarios, such as monitoring intersections in Dhaka, continuously streaming high-resolution video to cloud servers for traffic analysis consumes massive bandwidth and exposes the faces and license plates of citizens to potential interception. The proposed framework deploys a heavily pruned tracking architecture directly onto low-power edge devices (such as smart cameras or local IoT nodes). By processing the video locally and only transmitting anonymous tracking metadata (e.g., bounding box coordinates and object class), the system inherently preserves visual privacy while drastically reducing network latency.

### **Targeted Problem and Primary Beneficiaries**

The reliance on cloud computing for advanced Multi-Object Tracking introduces latency, wastes bandwidth, and creates severe privacy liabilities21. As data protection laws become stricter globally, surveillance systems must adapt. The beneficiaries include smart city administrators, law enforcement agencies seeking GDPR-compliant monitoring, and transport authorities monitoring public transit environments (e.g., Metro rail platforms) without violating passenger privacy22.

### **Literature Gap and Core Novelty**

While TinyML models exist, achieving the necessary accuracy for MOT on edge devices often requires aggressive network pruning. Conventional pruning techniques primarily emphasize simple parameter reduction, which often destroys the structural integrity of the neural network, leading to a massive drop in tracking accuracy, especially for small objects in dense crowds.  
The core novelty is adapting the MSP-Edge (Multi-Stage Pruning with Neuron Reconstruction) methodology for a continuous MOT pipeline23. Unlike standard iterative pruning, this approach separates optimization-oriented unstructured sparsification from deployment-oriented graph compaction. By reconstructing neurons post-pruning, the network retains its representational capacity for feature extraction while shedding redundant weights, allowing a complex YOLOv11+ByteTrack pipeline to run on highly constrained edge hardware with built-in data minimization architectures23.

### **Execution Strategy and Algorithmic Implementation**

> 1. **Baseline Training:** Train a YOLOv11n model on a crowd/traffic dataset.  
> 2. **Sparsification Phase:** Formulate pruning as a constrained optimization problem, applying non-convex regularization to promote weight sparsity dynamically during training without abrupt topological changes23.  
> 3. **Neuron Reconstruction:** Implement the deployment-oriented neuron reconstruction algorithm. This involves mathematically fusing surviving weights and compacting the computational graph to ensure that the theoretical sparsity translates into actual memory locality and execution efficiency on the edge device23.  
> 4. **Privacy Tracking Pipeline:** Integrate the pruned detector with a lightweight ByteTrack module. Ensure the software architecture deletes raw image frames immediately from RAM after the bounding box coordinates are extracted, transmitting only the metadata payload to the central server.

### **Performance Estimates and Risk Mitigation**

Applying this multi-stage pruning with neuron reconstruction is estimated to reduce model weights by approximately 55-60% and floating-point operations (FLOPs) by 60%, mirroring the success of MSP-Edge on older architectures24. Tracking accuracy (HOTA/MOTA) is expected to degrade by less than 2% compared to the unpruned baseline, a highly acceptable trade-off for edge deployment.  
A key risk is that high theoretical sparsity does not always translate into deployment-realized acceleration on hardware like the T4 GPU due to memory access patterns23. This is mitigated explicitly by the neuron reconstruction phase, which re-densifies the sparse graph into a compact, contiguous memory format optimized for the T4's Tensor Cores.

## **Deep Dive Execution Template: Evidential-ByteTrack**

The following template provides a rigorous, phase-by-phase architectural and operational blueprint for executing the top-ranked recommendation (**Evidential-ByteTrack**) over a 20-week (5-month) timeline. This schedule is scaled perfectly for a three-person undergraduate team utilizing a single Nvidia T4 GPU (e.g., Google Colab Pro tier).

### **Phase 1: Infrastructure Standardization and Baseline Replication (Weeks 1–4)**

**Objective:** Establish a stable, reproducible development environment, benchmark existing state-of-the-art architectures, and preprocess the massive datasets required for training.

> * **Data Pipeline Engineering:** The DriveIndia dataset contains over 66,000 images with 24 distinct classes, while IDD-3D provides complex unstructured annotations28. The team must write robust Python scripts to ingest these datasets, normalize the bounding box annotations into the standard YOLO format, and apply aggressive spatial augmentations (e.g., random cropping, mosaic augmentation) to simulate the chaotic occlusion patterns of Dhaka traffic.  
> * **Baseline Benchmarking:** Deploy standard YOLOv11n coupled with the baseline ByteTrack algorithm. Execute initial training and inference runs on the T4 GPU. Crucially, the team must compute and log the baseline HOTA, MOTA, and IDF1 metrics, as well as the inference FPS. This establishes the strict control group against which all algorithmic improvements will be measured31.  
> * **Theoretical Grounding:** Conduct a deep literature review specifically focusing on Dirichlet distribution mathematics, Dempster-Shafer theory, and subjective logic10. The team must fully comprehend the derivations of epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty to draft the mathematical foundation of the manuscript's methodology section.

**Checkpoint Deliverable:** A fully functional, reproducing baseline tracker operating at $>30$ FPS on the DriveIndia validation set, with all baseline metrics rigorously documented.

### **Phase 2: Evidential Head Formulation and PyTorch Integration (Weeks 5–9)**

**Objective:** Modify the internal architecture of the YOLO detection head to output evidential parameters, and train the uncertainty-aware detector from scratch.

> * **Architectural Modifications:** Access the source code of the chosen YOLO architecture (e.g., via the Ultralytics repository). The standard detection heads utilize Sigmoid or Softmax activation layers to output probabilities bounded between 0 and 1\. These must be replaced with a Softplus activation layer. The Softplus function ensures the generation of strictly non-negative evidence values ${e}_{i}$. From this output tensor, the Dirichlet concentration parameters are calculated computationally as ${\alpha }_{i}={e}_{i}+1$. The total evidence $S=\sum\limits_{}^{}{\alpha }_{i}$ and the resulting epistemic uncertainty $u=\frac{K}{S}$ are then derived as distinct tensor outputs.  
> * **Custom Loss Function Engineering:** Implement the Mean Squared Error Evidential Loss (${L}_{MSE-EDL}$) natively in PyTorch. This requires programming the exact mathematical formulation, ensuring numerical stability (avoiding division by zero or log of zero errors). Furthermore, program the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence regularizer to penalize evidence assigned to incorrect classes. Implement the delayed annealing factor ${\lambda }_{t}=\min\limits_{}(1.0,t/10)$ to multiply the KL term, ensuring gradient stability during the turbulent early epochs of training11.  
> * **T4 Compute Optimization:** The Nvidia T4 GPU is limited to 16GB of VRAM. To train on high-resolution DriveIndia images, the team must utilize PyTorch's Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP). By casting specific operations to FP16, the Turing architecture's Tensor Cores are engaged, effectively halving VRAM usage, allowing for larger batch sizes, and significantly accelerating the training of the new evidential head.

**Checkpoint Deliverable:** A trained Evidential-YOLO model capable of outputting bounding boxes alongside a calibrated uncertainty score $u\in [0,1]$ for each detection.

### **Phase 3: Association Algorithm Redesign (Weeks 10–14)**

**Objective:** Rewire the ByteTrack association matrix to ingest and utilize the newly quantified epistemic uncertainty during the object matching phase.

> * **Algorithmic Adaptations:** The standard ByteTrack protocol associates bounding boxes across frames using a bipartite matching algorithm (the Hungarian algorithm) based strictly on spatial IoU3. The team will modify this core logic. Extract the bounding box predictions alongside their newly generated uncertainty scores $u$.

> **The Evidential-IoU Matrix:** Replace the standard IoU distance matrix ${D}_{IoU}$ with the proposed Evidential-IoU matrix formulation:

> $$E-IoU(i,j)=IoU(i,j)\times \exp (-\beta \cdot {u}_{i})$$

> *   
>   Where $\beta$ is an empirically tuned scaling factor. The logic here is vital: if a vehicle is heavily occluded (yielding a high epistemic uncertainty ${u}_{i}$), the exponential term mathematically shrinks, which in turn reduces the penalty of a poor spatial IoU overlap. This prevents the Kalman filter from prematurely terminating the track due to a mathematically predictable, temporary occlusion.  
> * **Ablation Studies:** Systematically test varying values of the hyperparameter $\beta$. Run comparative evaluations matching the $E-IoU$ performance directly against standard IoU and Generalized IoU (GIoU) metrics on the DriveIndia test set.

**Checkpoint Deliverable:** A fully integrated Evidential-ByteTrack pipeline, optimized and validated through ablation studies.

### **Phase 4: Rigorous Evaluation and Qualitative Visualization (Weeks 15–17)**

**Objective:** Extract definitive, publication-ready metrics and generate highly persuasive visual qualitative proofs for journal reviewers.

> * **Quantitative Extraction:** Execute the standardized CLEAR MOT and HOTA evaluation scripts across the test datasets6. Tabulate the exact percentage improvements in Identity Switches (IDSW) and the Identity F1 Score (IDF1) compared to the baseline established in Phase 1\.  
> * **Qualitative Visualization Scripting:** Develop a custom OpenCV/Python script to render the tracking outputs on video clips of Dhaka or Indian traffic. Modify the bounding box drawing function to color-code the boxes based on their real-time epistemic uncertainty (e.g., rendering boxes in solid green when certainty is high, and shifting to a dashed red line when epistemic uncertainty spikes during an occlusion). Render side-by-side video comparisons showing the baseline tracker dropping IDs (failing) while the Evidential-ByteTrack successfully maintains IDs through severe occlusions. This visual evidence provides immediate, intuitive proof of the mathematical claims, which is highly persuasive for Q1 reviewers.

**Checkpoint Deliverable:** A complete suite of quantitative tables and qualitative video renderings demonstrating the superiority of the proposed framework.

### **Phase 5: Manuscript Drafting and Submission (Weeks 18–20)**

**Objective:** Synthesize the mathematical contributions, software engineering, and empirical findings into a structurally flawless, Q1-standard academic manuscript.

> * **Structuring the Manuscript:**  
  * *Introduction:* Frame the narrative around the unique complexities of Global South traffic heterogeneity and the fundamental limitations of deterministic edge trackers in handling non-linear occlusions.  
  * *Related Work:* Contrast the proposed method with computationally heavy Bayesian Neural Networks, TinyML architectures7, and prior iterations of ByteTrack3.  
  * *Proposed Architecture:* Detail the Dirichlet mathematical proofs extensively12. Clearly define how the modified Hungarian cost matrix formulations integrate this math.  
  * *Experiments:* Present the IDD and DriveIndia results using rigorous Markdown-style tables. Explicitly discuss the T4 compute envelope, proving the "cheap novelty" and real-world viability of the solution.  
  * *Conclusion:* Summarize the concrete reduction in IDSW and articulate the broader viability of evidential logic for edge-based surveillance and autonomous driving.  
> * **Target Selection and Open Science:** Submit the finalized manuscript to *IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems* or a similarly ranked computational vision journal. Concurrently, ensure the codebase is meticulously cleaned, documented, and uploaded to a public GitHub repository. Linking an open-source, reproducible codebase in the manuscript satisfies modern open-science mandates and significantly boosts the probability of acceptance by peer reviewers.
